OPERATION ORDER

FIELD MANUAL

THE LANE WAR

  • Command Points (CP) accrue over time. Spend them on troops and support. Holding lane flags raises income and drains enemy morale.
  • Click a unit card (or keys 1–6), then click a lane to deploy. Units advance and engage on their own.
  • Win by breaking enemy morale. Every soldier lost costs morale — US morale bleeds faster per casualty than VC morale.
  • Elevation matters. Troops firing downhill gain range and damage. Study the terrain line of each lane.
  • Cover is not concealment. Concealed VC troops in brush cannot be targeted until spotted — and their first volley from hiding hits harder.

COVER & TRENCHES

  • Your squads do not take cover on their own. Select one and tap a position, or press E, to move into it. When to move is the decision.
  • A rock holds one squad, a trench two, a long trench three. The pips under a position show how full it is — including an enemy position, once they crowd one.
  • Crowding is a trade. Squads packed into one position lose protection against rifles and take far more from a single shell. Massing wins the firefight and loses to the mortar that finds it.
  • Hold still and the position improves — men dig, and protection climbs for about thirteen seconds.
  • Keep holding and the enemy ranges you in. A round lands short as a warning, then the position starts taking accurate fire. Moving resets the clock — and it resets the digging too.
  • A trench conceals the men in it. You can see into your own; an enemy trench stays shut.
  • Rifles will not take a trench. A dug-in squad beats the same squad in the open almost every time — do not feed men into it frontally.
  • Weapons are drawn as they are. An SKS has no magazine under it, an AK does; the ARVN carry long wooden M14s and the M79 is a short fat tube. You can tell what is shooting at you by looking.
  • The Weapons Team carries an M79 (F) — high explosive out to more than twice grenade range. It is the infantry answer to a trench, and it costs no CP.
  • Explosives are the answer. A trench does not shelter you from a shell, it contains it: grenades and artillery hit HARDER against a dug position, and harder again if it is crowded. That is how you clear one.

SNIPERS

  • Snipers take time to aim — watch for the scope glint. One shot, one kill.
  • Two snipers aiming at each other triggers a duel: high ground and range decide who fires first.
  • Counter a sniper with artillery, smoke of battle... or your own marksman.

US — FIREPOWER DOCTRINE ★

  • Fire Mission: 105mm barrage on a marked point, after a delay.
  • Napalm: burns a strip of ground — and the brush concealing the enemy. Fire does not check dog tags; keep your own men clear.
  • Dustoff: medevac flight restores morale.
  • Air Cav: Huey inserts a squad anywhere on a lane.
  • Arc Light: B-52 strike. Devastating against massed troops, wasted on dispersed ones.
  • The APC is immune to rifle fire. Not resistant — immune. Rounds spark off the hull. Only rockets, grenades, satchels and shells will stop it.
  • Engineers clear traps and tunnels; LRRP recon spots concealed enemies at long range.

VC — GROUND DOCTRINE ★

  • Punji pits & mines: hidden traps placed on the lane. Engineers can find and clear them.
  • Spider hole: a buried marksman who fires one ambush shot, then vanishes again.
  • Tunnel: dig a forward exit — your infantry emerge there instead of walking the whole lane.
  • Guerrillas in brush are concealed: untargetable until spotted, with an ambush bonus on their first strike.
